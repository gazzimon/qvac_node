05 · The trust gap

A market needs someone who sees both ends. That is the problem.

Someone has to match each request to a node — and in doing so sees who asked and who answered. Someone has to meter consumption, apply the price, and decide what each provider is owed.

That someone reconstructs the entire activity of the network and holds the money. Encrypting the traffic doesn't prevent it: the coordinator has to read both sides to do its job.

You cannot have an ownerless market if the piece that decides and charges belongs to someone.

And it is not only privacy. A provider can inflate its own token count that far without detection — an honest $100 billed as $1,569 on the same query. The paper that measures it names in-enclave attestation as the way out. arXiv:2605.30040

Four jobs, and what each one leaks
matching links a buyer to a seller — both identities, every request
metering volume and shape of everything anyone consumes
settlement who gets paid what — and the power to tilt it
reputation who stays in the market and who is quietly buried

Transport, discovery and verification between nodes are solved for us. This centre is what the protocol cannot solve on its own.
